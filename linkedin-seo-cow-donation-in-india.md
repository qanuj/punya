# Cow Donation in India: What Your Gau Daan Actually Buys

*Draft LinkedIn article. Voice: "we" (Punya Charitable Trust).*

---

You can donate a cow in India for ₹21. You can also do it for ₹1,08,000.

Both are real prices, from the same list. The gap tells you something most guides on **cow donation in India** skip: Gau Daan is no longer one act. It is a menu of specific things a cow needs, and knowing what each one buys is the difference between giving and guessing.

We run Punya Charitable Trust, a cow-welfare trust working from Patkhauli Gaushala in Ghazipur, Uttar Pradesh. Here is how we think about giving a cow, and what we think most pages on the subject leave out.

## What is cow donation (Gau Daan)?

Gau Daan means giving a cow, or giving for a cow's care. It sits deep in Hindu tradition. Pages like the one from [Doon Animal Welfare Foundation](https://doonanimalwelfarefoundation.org/cow-donation-in-india/) trace it to the idea of the cow as Kamadhenu, the wish-granting divine cow, and to Lord Krishna's life as a cowherd. People traditionally give at weddings, births and memorial ceremonies.

The tradition is old. The practical situation today is different. Almost nobody hands a live cow to a stranger anymore. They fund a gaushala, a shelter, to look after cows on their behalf.

## Why gaushalas depend on donors

A gaushala (*go* = cow, *shala* = shelter) is a home for cows, calves and oxen, many of them abandoned, injured or past milking age. According to [Wikipedia's entry on Goshala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goshala), government grants and donations are the primary income of these shelters, and for some, donations are the only source.

That is the plain reason your gift matters. A shelter does not stop needing money when the festival is over. Fodder, medicine and wages are daily costs.

## The ways to donate, and how to choose

Most organisations offer a similar set of routes:

- **Adopt a cow.** A recurring sponsorship of one animal's care. [Sur Shyam Gaushala](https://www.surshyam.co/) lists ₹21,000 a year to adopt a cow.
- **One-time campaigns** for a rescue or a treatment.
- **Monthly giving.** [Doon Animal Welfare Foundation](https://doonanimalwelfarefoundation.org/) offers a "Give Every Month" subscription.
- **Tax benefit.** Several organisations, such as [Krishnayan](https://www.krishnayan.com/cmp/donation-public), say donations qualify under Section 80G. Always confirm this with the organisation before you give.

Some organisations are huge. [Shri Mataji Gaushala](https://www.matajigaushala.org/cow-donetion) says it shelters 65,000+ cows on a 250-acre campus in Barsana. Others are small and local. Neither is automatically better. Size tells you about scale. It does not tell you what happens to your rupee.

## What we think is missing: a line between your money and one cow

Reading through what ranks for this topic, the pattern is clear. Most pages explain the spiritual meaning well. Many show impressive totals. Few let you see **what a specific amount does** or **which cow it reached**.

That is the gap we are trying to close at Punya.

**1. A price for every kind of care.** Our seva list is public. A few examples:

- ₹21 buys one brick toward shelter construction
- ₹251 covers a Gau Puja
- ₹301 a month is the First Roti for Gau Mata seva
- ₹1,100 funds Gau Chikitsa Daan: medicines, first aid and treatment
- ₹3,000 a month sponsors the care of one named cow through Adopt a Cow
- ₹11,000 funds a Gau Ambulance, for emergency rescue transport
- ₹51,000 is a Real Gau Daan, a non-symbolic cow donation

The point is not to sell you a number. It is that you can read what each number is for before you pay.

**2. Cows with names.** On our Gaumata page, every cow in our care is named and accounted for, and each has a story. A recent one is Harihar, who needs help with a new beginning. A named animal is harder to lose track of than a headcount.

**3. A record you can follow.** Donors connect through the Punya app, which records every seva with daily photos from the gaushala. Sponsors of an individual cow receive monthly photo updates.

## What to ask before you donate to any gaushala

This applies to us as much as anyone:

1. **Where exactly are the cows?** A named place, not just a region.
2. **What does my amount cover?** Fodder, medicine, shelter, wages: it should say.
3. **Can I see the work?** Photos, updates, or a visit.
4. **Is there a tax receipt, and under what section?** Ask for it in writing.
5. **Are the books open?** Annual reports, audited accounts.

On the last point, we owe you honesty. Our transparency page promises "every rupee accounted for, every day" and is built around a live dashboard. Today that dashboard is still filling in, and audited reports are available on request. We would rather say that plainly than overstate it. Accountability is something we are building in public, and you should hold us to it.

## Give a cow what it needs, not just what feels symbolic

The tradition behind Gau Daan is about care, respect and nurture. We take that literally. A cow does not need a gesture. It needs grain, green fodder, a vet, warmth in winter and someone to notice when it is sick.

If you are searching for how to do cow donation in India, start with what you want your money to do, then find the shelter that will show you it happened.
